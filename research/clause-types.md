# Clause types that burn people (Redline research)

Date: 2026-11-09. Agent: clause-types.

**Read this first.** The ranking below is my judgment of the sources I found. It is not a measured league table. No single dataset counts complaints by clause type across contracts, leases, freelance agreements and ToS. Each row rests on a different kind of evidence (complaint counts, share of workers affected, clause prevalence, survey incidence, enforcement actions). Those measures are not directly comparable. Rank was set by (a) whether a regulator or survey gives a volume or incidence number, and (b) how large that number is. Rows 5 and 6 have weak or no frequency evidence, so their order is soft.

## Notes per finding

**1. Auto-renewal.** Verbatim from the FTC release: "the FTC receives thousands of complaints about negative option and recurring subscription practices each year" and "in 2024 the Commission received nearly 70 consumer complaints per day on average, up from 42 per day in 2021." The complaint trend is rising. URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring. Caveat: the rule itself may no longer be in force. A search summary said a federal appeals court vacated it in 2025, but that came from the search tool's own recollection and I did not verify it. The complaint figures do not depend on the rule.

**2. Non-payment and late payment.** Verbatim from the Freelancers Union post: "sixty-two percent of freelance workers based in New York had lost wages at least once in their career," "91% of survey respondents reported experiencing late or overdue wages at least once in their career," and "54% reporting experiencing delays of 3 months or longer." The page does not give a sample size. A separate 2015 Freelancers Union national report (5,358 respondents, 81% of those with payment trouble paid late) appeared only in a search snippet; I did not open it. This is a payment-behaviour problem. The sources do not tie it to specific clause wording. URL: https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2022/05/12/over-60-of-ny-freelancers-report-not-being-paid-for-work-performed

**3. Non-competes.** Verbatim from the FTC release: "An estimated 30 million workers—nearly one in five Americans—are subject to a noncompete." Other estimates in search results ranged from at least 18% (a DOL study) to about 50% (EPI); I did not open those. The FTC rule was reported as being abandoned in September 2025 (NPR, search snippet only: https://www.npr.org/2025/09/05/nx-s1-5528937/ftc-noncompete-ban-trump). URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes

**4. Arbitration and class waivers.** From the CFPB page (archived, preliminary 2013 findings): "Roughly 9 out of 10 arbitration clauses allow banks to block consumers from joining class actions." "Only 7.7 percent of banks use arbitration clauses for checking accounts, but 62 percent of the top 50 banks do." Only two class filings in arbitration were found, against more than 400 federal credit-card class actions. Fewer than 1,250 consumer arbitrations were filed in 2010 to 2012. These figures cover financial products only. The CFPB's 2017 rule on this was disapproved by Congress. URL: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-finds-few-consumers-file-arbitration-cases/

**5. Landlord late fees and junk fees.** The Massachusetts AG secured a $500,000 settlement with a property manager over illegal fees including lease renewal and move-in fees (URL in table). The DC AG says late fees are capped at 5% of monthly rent and cannot be charged before 5 days after the due date (https://oag.dc.gov/node/2735). A bipartisan group of state AGs asked the FTC to act on hidden rental fees (https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/bipartisan_group_state_attorneys_general_urge_ftc_combat_hidden_rental_fees_0426.html). These show enforcement activity, not frequency. I read them only through search summaries. The NCLC report (https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/202409_Report_What-the-Heck-Dude_Exec-Summary.pdf) may hold numbers, but the PDF would not parse, so I cannot quote it.

**6. Unilateral changes to terms.** The ABA's Business Law Today says traditional contract doctrine forbids unilateral modification and that modification clauses are the usual workaround (https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2016-may/online-contracts). That is a search summary, not a quote. The "Empty Promises" page returned HTTP 403, so the 19% credit card figure is unverified. Treat this row as a flag that the clause exists, not as a frequency claim.

## What I could not find

- **Any cross-clause complaint ranking.** No FTC, CFPB or state AG dataset I found breaks complaints down by contract clause. FTC Consumer Sentinel (6.5 million reports in 2024) sorts by scam or industry category, not clause (https://www.ftc.gov/reports/consumer-sentinel-network-data-book-2024; seen via search summary only).
- **Liability caps, indemnity, IP assignment / work-for-hire, termination without cause.** No frequency or complaint data found for any of them. One search on freelancer IP clauses returned only a passing advocate quote and no survey. I am not ranking them.
- **Fee escalators** (rent or subscription price increases). No data found. The only related item is the unverified 19% figure in row 6.
- **Late-payment penalty clauses** as a clause type, as distinct from late payment as an outcome. No source separates them.
- **Forum threads** (Reddit, etc.). I did not search for them within the budget, so there is no forum evidence at all.
- **Second sources.** Most figures rest on a single source, and several sources were read only as search summaries (flagged above).
- **Current legal status** of the FTC click-to-cancel and non-compete rules. Not verified.

# Existing contract-review tools (research for Redline)

Note: most vendors do not publish prices. Third-party figures are estimates and often come from competitors' blogs; they are flagged as such.

## 1. Spellbook
- What it does: AI contract drafting, review and redlining inside Microsoft Word.
- Target: Law firms and in-house legal teams; solo practitioners and mid-size firms (per HyperStart).
- Price: No published pricing; custom quote, 7-day free trial. Third-party estimates (HyperStart, a competing CLM vendor): about $20/user/mo entry, about $40 team (annual), about $350/user/mo enterprise with a 6-month minimum. The estimates conflict across sources and are unverified.
- Most common complaint: Pricing opacity and cost. Also no full CLM features (approval workflows, renewal tracking). Review aggregator summaries also cite inconsistent output. Review ratings differ: G2 4.7 (28 reviews) vs Trustpilot 2.5 (10 reviews), per search summary (small samples).
- Source URLs: https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/spellbook-pricing/ ; https://www.g2.com/products/spellbook

## 2. DoNotPay
- What it does: Consumer "robot lawyer" subscription (disputes, letters, legal documents). The FTC said it could not deliver on its promise to generate valid legal documents or replace a lawyer.
- Target: Consumers and small businesses.
- Price: $36 every two months for the consumer subscription at the time of the FTC complaint; "Small Business Protection Plan" $49.99/month. Current pricing: not found from a primary source.
- Most common complaint: Overstated AI-lawyer claims. FTC settlement of $193,000 (finalized January 2025). It alleged the company did not test its chatbot against human lawyers and did not retain lawyers. A competitor's blog (unverified) cites unexpected-renewal complaints.
- Source URLs: https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/robot-lawyer-website-donotpay-settles-ftc-claims-it-couldnt-deliver-on-promises ; https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/DoNotPayInc-APCOAPC.pdf ; https://ailawyer.pro/blog/donotpay-alternatives (competitor, unverified)

## 3. LegalOn
- What it does: AI contract review against playbooks, plus an AI assistant.
- Target: In-house legal teams, small legal teams up to enterprise.
- Price: Custom, user-based; the vendor says it is not priced per review. Third-party estimates, unverified: $3,000-$8,000/yr for small teams, or about $6,600/user/yr.
- Most common complaint: Reviews are very positive (G2 5.0 from 10 reviews). The few cons are limited integrations, manual cleanup on highly bespoke documents, and contract-type options not matching a reviewer's documents. No dominant complaint exists in this small sample.
- Source URLs: https://www.g2.com/products/legalon/pricing ; https://g2.com/products/legalon/reviews ; https://www.legalontech.com/post/legalon-pricing (vendor)

## 4. Terms of Service; Didn't Read (ToS;DR)
- What it does: Volunteer-curated ratings (Class A to E) of websites' terms of service and privacy policies. Points are tagged good or bad and scored 1 to 10.
- Target: General public and consumers.
- Price: Not stated on its About page, which says it is funded by non-profits and donations; its data and software are open. (Search summaries also describe it as free and open source.)
- Most common complaint: Limited coverage. Many services have no class yet, the editing site is in an early phase, and support depends on volunteers. No user-review complaint source found; this limitation comes from the project's own page.
- Source URLs: https://edit.tosdr.org/about ; https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/terms-service-too-long-site-reads-them-for-you-flna931724

## 5. Harvey
- What it does: Generative AI platform for legal work, including contract analysis, research and drafting.
- Target: Large law firms and enterprise legal departments.
- Price: Not published. Third-party estimates, unverified: about $1,200/seat/month, with minimums of roughly 20-25 seats on 12-month terms. Sources disagree on the minimum.
- Most common complaint: Opaque, high pricing and hallucination risk. Harvey's own benchmark claims about 0.2% hallucination (vendor figure). Commentary notes lawyers are still sanctioned for AI errors. One unverified report of a fabricated citation.
- Source URLs: https://www.fast.io/resources/harvey-ai-review-2026/ ; https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/harvey-ai-price-increase-per-seat (not opened; the review page 404'd) ; https://harvey.ai/blog/biglaw-bench-hallucinations ; https://ediscoverytoday.com/2026/06/04/attorneys-are-blaming-legal-ai-technology-vendors-for-hallucinations-artificial-intelligence-trends/

## 6. Ironclad
- What it does: Contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform with AI features: workflows, repository, review.
- Target: Mid-market and enterprise legal and business teams.
- Price: Not published (G2 says none provided). One competitor blog (HyperStart) says it is costly compared to other CLMs.
- Most common complaint: Opaque and high pricing, with some onboarding friction (RFP.wiki summary, an aggregator's synthesis, not individual reviews). Gartner Peer Insights rating is 4.7 across 286 reviews.
- Source URLs: https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/contract-life-cycle-management/vendor/ironclad/product/ironclad-clm ; https://rfp.wiki/vendors/ironclad ; https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/ironclad-reviews/

## 7. General chatbots (ChatGPT etc.)
- What it does: General LLMs used ad hoc to explain or review contracts. No contract-review-specific data found.
- Target: Anyone.
- Price: Not researched; not found.
- Most common complaint: Hallucination and unreliable legal answers. The Stanford RegLab/HAI study found 69-88% hallucination rates on specific legal queries for models then tested (GPT-3.5, PaLM 2, Llama 2). It measured legal questions, not contract review. A follow-up study found legal-specific research tools erred in more than 17% of cases.
- Source URLs: https://hai.stanford.edu/news/hallucinating-law-legal-mistakes-large-language-models-are-pervasive

## 8. LawGeex (defunct as a brand)
- What it does: AI contract review against company policies for enterprises.
- Target: Enterprise legal teams.
- Price: Never published; custom (per directory listings). No free plan found.
- Most common complaint: not found. The company was broken up in 2023: Robin AI bought assets and LegalSifter acquired the enterprise clients.
- Source URLs: https://www.legalsifter.com/news/legalsifter-acquires-lawgeex-enterprise-business ; https://softwarefinder.com/legal/lawgeex

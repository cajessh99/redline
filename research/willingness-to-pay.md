# Willingness to Pay: contract comprehension (Redline)

Quotes are verbatim from pages fetched. Items marked "(search summary only)" were seen only in a search-result summary, not on the page itself.

## Findings

1. Segment: freelancers and small businesses (pain and cost barrier)
   - Evidence: "Freelancers and small businesses can't afford attorney fees, and so choose to write their own risky contracts" / "or go without a contract at all, leading to lawsuits when things inevitably go awry." (a 2018 journalist's claim, not measured data)
   - Source: https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/31/freelancers-rights-come-of-age-as-gig-economy-booms

2. Segment: freelancers (free alternative exists)
   - Evidence: Freelancers Union says "accessing legal assistance can be time consuming and extremely costly." It offers "a free one-on-one legal consultation with an in-house attorney" to NYC freelancers, funded by the New York City Council. This is a free substitute that caps willingness to pay in that niche.
   - Source: https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2024/04/30/get-free-legal-assistance-with-the-new-freelancers-legal-clinic/

3. Segment: freelancers, startups, general contract review (high-end lawyer price)
   - Evidence: One California attorney's page lists "$300 flat fee" for a written consultation, "$1,000 flat fee" for Contract Review / Redline, and "$300 per hour" for additional review time.
   - Source: https://www.terms.law/services/employment-agreement-review/

4. Segment: general (lawyer hourly rates)
   - Evidence: "The average hourly rate for a contract lawyer to draft and review a contract is $150-$350." Flat fee: "Some attorneys also prefer charging a flat fee, ranging between $200-$800." For employment: "the lawyer charges $100-$200 for a standard employment contract review." Complex or dispute work: "$250-$1,000 per hour."
   - Source: https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-much-does-a-lawyer-cost

5. Segment: employees signing offers and non-competes (flat fees; search summary only)
   - Evidence: The summaries cite ContractsCounsel marketplace data of about $430 average for an employment contract review, about $340 for a non-compete review and $407 for a severance review. They also cite one firm's $175 flat fee for a standard California review, and marketplace bids of $240 to $800 in New York. These are vendor and marketplace figures; I did not read the pages.
   - Source: https://www.contractscounsel.com/b/employment-contract-review and https://terms.law/Employment-Contract-Review-Hub/ (not fetched; the ContractsCounsel site blocked automated fetching)

6. Segment: small business owners (skip lawyers because of cost; search summary only)
   - Evidence: A LegalShield-sponsored 2013 study reportedly found nearly 60% of owners with a significant legal event did not hire an attorney, citing high cost, and contract review was among the most-sought services. Average owner legal spend was reported as about $7,600 per year. The study is over a decade old and the sponsor sells legal plans. A 2025 LegalShield-related study is headlined "Yet Most Forgo Counsel" (page returned 403).
   - Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250519395084/en/New-Study-Legal-Pitfalls-Dent-Small-Business-Owners-Bottom-Line-Yet-Most-Forgo-Counsel

7. Segment: renters and consumers (AI tool price points; free tiers dominate; search summary only)
   - Evidence: Vendor and blog pages list lease-review tools at "free basic tier, $29/month for full analysis" (goHeather), Claude Pro at $20/month, free first review (LeaseLenses), and a free app with optional premium (RentGuard). The same guide says many tenant advocacy organizations offer free lease review. This suggests a free-tier and subscription norm, not per-document pricing. Prices are vendor claims.
   - Source: https://beginnersinai.org/ai-for-renters/ and https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761261184 (not fetched)

8. Contrary evidence: small businesses doubt that lawyers are worth the cost (UK, 2015)
   - Evidence: "Only 13% of small businesses agreed that lawyers 'provide a cost-effective means to resolve legal issues.'" However, the article also says fewer than 16% named cost as the most important factor when choosing a provider (reputation 30%, specialist knowledge 25%). It reports 53% of lawyer users agreed it was "easy to find a suitable legal services provider that I can afford." UK data, so transferability to the US is uncertain.
   - Source: https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/lawyers-still-not-cost-effective-small-businesses-tell-biggest-ever-survey

9. Contrary evidence: consumers use AI mostly for convenience, not price, and many still insist on lawyers for serious matters
   - Evidence: Rev survey: "Nearly two-thirds of Americans (65%) have already turned to an AI chatbot for legal help." Only 8% of users named the reason as "cheaper than consulting a lawyer," against 18% who cited convenience or accessibility. 41% "would only rely on a lawyer — not AI — to learn about serious matters." Vendor-run survey.
   - Source: https://webflow.rev.com/blog/ai-legal-advice-index

10. Segment: small business owners (free or cheap AI substitution; search summary only)
    - Evidence: A Smallpdf survey of 1,000 US professionals is reported to find 55% of business owners used AI to draft, edit or review contracts and 25% skip legal review to save money or time. 80% would still want a lawyer to review a contract over $100,000. Vendor survey; methodology not seen.
    - Source: https://smallpdf.com/blog/ai-contracts-in-small-business (page fetch returned only a title) and https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/11/everyones-using-ai-for-contracts-but.html

## Segments with the sharpest evidence of pain
Freelancers and small businesses have the most evidence of cost-driven skipping of review (findings 1, 2, 6, 10). Employees with offers or non-competes have the clearest price anchors, a few hundred dollars per review (findings 3 to 5). Renters appear mainly in free or low-priced tool markets (finding 7); I found no direct renter willingness-to-pay data.

## What I could not find
- No stated willingness-to-pay figure from any end user (freelancer, renter, employee, founder) for an AI contract explainer.
- No survey that measures the share of freelancers, renters or employees who skip review because of price. Findings 1 and 6 are an assertion and an old sponsored study.
- No price for a human lease review for renters, and no ToS-reading willingness-to-pay data.
- No startup-founder-specific data.
- Forum threads (MetaFilter, IndieTalk) returned 403, so I have no first-hand user comments on "too expensive."
- Primary pages for ContractsCounsel, LegalShield 2025 and Smallpdf were not readable; those figures are secondhand via search summaries.
- No market size figures were sought or invented.
